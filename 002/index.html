What Remains

On Feelings That Cannot Be Captured

A REFLECTION

The notion of remembrance has been top of mind as of late. Not nostalgia exactly. More the strange feeling of looking back at a period of time and realizing that, for whatever reason, you were fully there for it. The Last Resort feels like that to me. I'm grateful for the work I made there, but I'm equally grateful for the time itself. The meals. The conversations. The walks. The slow mornings. Making things without knowing exactly what they would become. Being around other people who were also trying to make sense of something. There is something comforting about knowing that the time was well spent, even if I can't quite explain what came from it. Since returning to New York, I've been noticing things differently. Recently, I discovered People's NY through a profile in Teeth Magazine. It felt like a breath of fresh air. Not just because of the art, but because of the way the space allows art to actually live. The skylight. The draping. The curtain hooks. The candlelit atmosphere.It feels like an alternative to the white box without having to announce itself as one. An ideal place for a sensual makeout.There was a small painting by Wes Aderhold of distorted busts that held my gaze. I kept returning to it. Maybe because I've been thinking about something similar in my own work. What I've been pursuing in my photographs are actually feelings that you can't quite depict. How do you photograph something that can't be captured? Or doesn't want to be depicted? I've spent years making photographs of figures, objects, surfaces, gestures, and light. Things that are physically there. But increasingly, I'm interested in what exists just outside of the image. The feeling left behind. The sensation of having been somewhere. The intimacy of a moment that can't be reconstructed. The thing you remember but can't quite describe. Maybe that's part of what contentment means to me right now, not needing to hold onto everything. Sometimes it is enough to have been there. To have spent the time well. To remember.

Since returning to New York, I've been noticing things differently. Recently, I discovered People's NY through a profile in Teeth Magazine. It felt like a breath of fresh air. Not just because of the art, but because of the way the space allows art to actually live. The skylight. The draping. The curtain hooks. Maybe because I've been thinking about something similar in my own work. What I've been pursuing in my photographs are actually feelings that you can't quite depict.

How do you photograph something that can't be captured? Or doesn't want to be depicted?

I've spent years making photographs of figures, objects, surfaces, gestures, and light. Things that are physically there. But increasingly, I'm interested in what exists just outside of the image.

The feeling left behind. The sensation of having been somewhere. The intimacy of a moment that can't be reconstructed. The thing you remember but can't quite describe.

Maybe that's part of what contentment means to me right now, not needing to hold onto everything. Sometimes it is enough to have been there.

To have spent the time well. To remember.
03 — STUDIO NOTES
I'm still carrying Baltimore with me.

The photographs, paintings, ceramics, and The Pleasure Table are continuing to unfold, but I'm also trying to preserve something less tangible from that time: the feeling of having enough room to notice what was happening.

Maybe that's what I'm really interested in making and protecting.

The world outside of the studio is full of distractions. I find some contentment in knowing that the work will never be a 1:1 representation of the feelings and spaces I'm trying to hold onto, but the effort to get there is worth the journey.

Traces of the feeling that something was pleasurable.

Something happened here.

And I was here to experience it.
